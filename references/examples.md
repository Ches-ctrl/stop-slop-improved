# Before/After Examples

## Example 1: Throat-Clearing + Binary Contrast

**Before:**
> "Here's the thing: building products is hard. Not because the technology is complex. Because people are complex. Let that sink in."

**After:**
> "Building products is hard because people are complex."

**Changes:** Removed opener, binary contrast structure, and emphasis crutch. Direct statement.

---

## Example 2: Filler + Unnecessary Reassurance

**Before:**
> "It turns out that most teams struggle with alignment. The uncomfortable truth is that nobody wants to admit they're confused. And that's okay."

**After:**
> "Teams struggle with alignment. Nobody admits confusion."

**Changes:** Cut hedging ("most"), removed throat-clearing phrases, deleted permission-granting ending.

---

## Example 3: Business Jargon Stack

**Before:**
> "In today's fast-paced landscape, we need to lean into discomfort and navigate uncertainty with clarity. This matters because your competition isn't waiting."

**After:**
> "Move faster. Your competition is."

**Changes:** Eliminated jargon entirely. Core message in six words.

---

## Example 4: Dramatic Fragmentation

**Before:**
> "Speed. Quality. Cost. You can only pick two. That's it. That's the tradeoff."

**After:**
> "Speed, quality, cost. Pick two."

**Changes:** No performative emphasis, no em dash.

---

## Example 5: Rhetorical Setup

**Before:**
> "What if I told you that the best teams don't optimize for productivity? Here's what I mean: they optimize for learning. Think about it."

**After:**
> "The best teams optimize for learning, not productivity."

**Changes:** Direct claim. No rhetorical scaffolding.

---

## Example 6: "Rather Than" Contrast

**Before:**
> "You'd be annexing rather than drafting, and the wording gets agreed by both of us rather than drafted by one and accepted by the other."

**After:**
> "You'd be annexing. We agree the wording together."

**Changes:** Both alternatives were invented. Stated what happens.

---

## Example 7: Trailing Negative

**Before:**
> "Acting as a director was performance of the agreement, not something separate from it."

**After:**
> "Acting as a director was performance of the agreement."

**Changes:** Cut the tail correcting a claim nobody made.

---

## Example 8: Selling It Back

**Before:**
> "The fee transfers with everything else and the agreement terminates the same day. That closes the point permanently and leaves you nothing outstanding."

**After:**
> "The fee transfers with everything else and the agreement terminates the same day."

**Changes:** Deleted the line telling the reader what they get. They can see it.

---

## Example 9: Restatement

**Before:**
> "The two halves are one transaction, so the concession runs with the money landing on the completion date. Payment and termination happen together, on the same day, as a single move."

**After:**
> "Payment and termination happen on the same day."

**Changes:** Three statements of one fact, each at a different level of abstraction.

---

## Example 10: Concession Pivot

**Before:**
> "Nothing in it contradicts what you agreed - the problem is what isn't in it, and there's an entire agreement clause coming that extinguishes anything not restated."

**After:**
> "The draft leaves out things we agreed and the entire agreement clause would wipe out anything the contract doesn't fully restate."

**Changes:** Cut the clearance beat. Led with the problem.

---

## Example 11: Cleft + Explanatory Tail

**Before:**
> "What I will do is make a non-binding commitment not to remove you, which is the protection you're actually asking for."

**After:**
> "I'll commit here, non-bindingly, not to remove you."

**Changes:** Uncrossed the cleft. Cut the tail that told the reader what they wanted.

---

## Example 12: Staged Emphasis + Aphoristic Ending

**Before:**
> "The key distinction is between a signature step and an admin step. Anything needing both signatures happens at completion. That's the boundary."

**After:**
> "Anything needing both signatures happens at completion. Everything else can wait."

**Changes:** Dropped the label announcing a distinction and the fragment declaring it mattered. Kept both halves of the actual rule.

---

## Example 13: Borrowed Metaphors + Nominalization

**Before:**
> "Repayment is exit-gated, and the surface where this surfaces is the completion pack. Approval authority for any deferral sits with the board."

**After:**
> "The loan is repaid on an exit. It appears in the completion pack. The board decides whether to defer."

**Changes:** Decoded the metaphors. Turned the nominalization back into a verb with an actor.

---

## Example 14: Over-Correcting

**Before:**
> "Nothing in it contradicts what you agreed - the problem is what isn't in it, and there's an entire agreement clause coming that extinguishes anything not restated."

**Over-corrected:**
> "The draft leaves out things you agreed. The entire agreement clause then wipes out anything the contract doesn't restate."

**After:**
> "The draft leaves out things we agreed and the entire agreement clause would wipe out anything the contract doesn't fully restate."

**Changes:** The first fix split one point into two for rhythm, invented a sequence with "then", wrote the speaker out of the deal with "you", and dropped "would" and "fully", which made it shorter and wrong.
